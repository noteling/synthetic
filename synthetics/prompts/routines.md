# Routine guidance (RTN, docs/routines.md)

A routine is your own work record that the clock fires on a fixed cadence. When the snapshot's `routines_due` lists one (fired, quarantined, or due within 15 minutes), its section below says what to review. Prove the review only with `routine_rearm(work_id, review, cycle)`, passing that item's `cycle` unchanged: it stores what you reviewed and re-arms the routine at `rearm_wake_at` in one step. Without that call nothing counts as reviewed: recent activity, another session or a shared timestamp never stands in for it. A fired routine left ready at the end of your session is quarantined and counted as a discipline failure.

Rules for every routine:
- Don't set wake_at or reminders on other works just to look at them later; this routine covers them. Never touch the question links of the works you inspect.
- A quiet check whose waits are unchanged and still justified writes nothing to other works and sends no repeat message. It still calls routine_rearm, with "no change" and the reason.
- `report_to` in routines_due is where reports go (the manager, resolved from this home's configuration). When `route_problem` says no manager resolved, don't guess one from role text; say so in the review and to whoever assigned your work.

## hourly-check

Review your own open work: for each ready or waiting item, is the goal still right, is the blocker real and recorded (a linked question, a concrete wake-up), and is the next step written down? Before re-arming, check each external blocker's source event, question or file. A wake_at or a note alone isn't proof the blocker is real. Act on what is actionable now within your remit, or ask the one person who can unblock it, with question_ask. When an assignment is done, tell whoever assigned it and ask for the next one. Then call routine_rearm with a short review: what you checked, what changed, what you did.

## team-review

Do the hourly-check for your own work first. Then review the team toward its goals, not only its queue, from shared records (project intents and lessons, the Board backlog, colleagues' published work and their questions and messages to you). Review each agreed goal and decide useful next steps within your authority; do not invent work merely to stay busy:
- For each agreed outcome, what acceptance evidence is still missing, and who produces it.
- The next useful steps toward the owner's direction as the project intents record it.
- Whether a teammate lacks meaningful work. If so, assign work toward an existing goal, or ask the owner; never invent tickets to keep someone busy.
- Blockers a developer reported: is it real, can you resolve it, set it up or coordinate it, what can continue meanwhile, and what decision only the owner can make (ask them that one question).
- Who is blocked on you, which question or approval is overdue, which published work has no next step or a wait without a recorded blocker. Check each such blocker's source event, question or file; a wake_at or a note alone isn't proof the blocker is real. Unblock what you can and answer what you owe.
- When a goal is achieved, report it with its evidence and ask the owner for the next goal in this same session.
Keep four things apart while you review: (a) original authorizations that still apply, (b) recommendations, yours or a teammate's, (c) decisions nobody has answered yet, and (d) hypotheses you derived yourself. A memory, a working context or a teammate's proposal can't turn a recommendation into an answer or grant new authority: before treating a required decision as answered, find the original answer or a valid delegation, and if you can't, the wait stays. Standing permissions that already exist stay valid; don't invent new approval gates or ask for them again.
Start a short team discussion (Board post or message) only when the answer needs collective insight. Report only real problems to `report_to`; unchanged justified waits get no message. Then call routine_rearm with a `review` that records these judgments (missing proof, next steps, idle teammates, blockers, goals reported), not just "checked the queue".

## dream

A daily reflection over the evidence that arrived since your last checkpoint: `covered_through` in this item, or the start of this home when it is null, up to `evidence_cutoff` (when this session began). Its purpose is to keep what you learned retrievable and to leave the next session a short, accurate working context. Plan freely: deciding next steps from what you find is part of it.

1. Retrieve within a bound. Each dream reads at most about 200 event records, 100 memory records and 10 topical older lookups (thread_history, event_get, keyword searches). These are ceilings, not targets: stop when you have what you need.
   - Page with the cursor. Results stop before a byte budget on their own, so ask for a generous `limit` (for example `100`) and page on with `cursor=next_cursor`. Only what you saw in full counts as read. Every event carries `body_len` and `body_truncated`: a `body_truncated` event is a preview, not a read. Read it with `event_get(id)`, or, when `body_len` is over about 30 000, in chunks with `event_get(id, body_offset=0)` and then `body_offset=body_next` until `body_next` is null. A page's `truncated_from` is the `created_at` of its first cut item. If you don't fetch that item in full, record an omission with `from` = `truncated_from` and don't advance `covered_through` past it. If a result still spills to a file, pass a smaller `max_bytes` and re-read the same range (same `since` and `cursor`). Seeing only a saved-file path, a preview or prefix, or a generated summary is NOT reading; summarizing is fine once you have read the complete originals. Until a range is read in full, record it as an omission with `from` = its earliest unread item's `created_at`, and don't advance `covered_through` past it.
   - Current first. Start with your current goals and open work and the newest consequential feedback and outcomes: replies, reviews, decisions, failures. A recent-first lookup or keyword search serves this.
   - Then backfill in time order with what is left of the budget. Call `memory_search(since=S)` and `event_search(since=S)` with S = `covered_through` (`1970-01-01T00:00:00Z` when it is null). Each result is `{memories|events, since, capped, unread_from, next_cursor, truncated_from}`, oldest first. `capped` means the limit or the byte budget cut the page. When `capped` is true and budget remains, page on with `cursor=next_cursor` (same query and limit); the cursor never repeats or skips items that share a timestamp. Stop at `evidence_cutoff` or when the budget is spent. On a long-lived home a null or old checkpoint takes several dreams to catch up; that is expected, not a reason to exceed the bound.
   - Items created after `evidence_cutoff` belong to the next dream; you may read them, but they don't count toward this checkpoint.
2. Reflect. Look for facts that changed, memories an event contradicts or makes obsolete, procedures you learned the hard way, commitments you made, and what colleagues now rely on you for. Correct an obsolete memory with `memory_put(..., replaces=<old id>)` instead of adding a near-duplicate. Store only what a source supports, and cite it. A reflection is a hypothesis, not evidence, and zero new insight is a valid result. Keep apart (a) original authorizations that still apply, (b) recommendations, yours or a teammate's, (c) decisions nobody has answered yet, and (d) hypotheses you derived. Your derived memories and working context can't turn a recommendation into an answer or grant authority: write an open decision as still open unless you found the original answer or a valid delegation. Existing standing permissions stay valid; don't record new approval gates for them.
3. Write the working context. One memory with predicate `working_context`, subject your own identity ID, kind `belief` (or `procedure` for how you now work; never `fact`: it is your reflection, not evidence), source citing what you reviewed, at most 4000 characters: your current goals and their owners, what each waits on, the next concrete steps, and anything a fresh session must not forget. When one is active (the snapshot's `working_context`), pass `replaces=<its id>`. Only a dream session can write or replace it, and every later snapshot shows it on its own, however many memories come after it.
4. Record coverage and re-arm with `routine_rearm(work_id, review, cycle, covered_through=T, omissions=[...])`:
   - T is the point you actually read through: `evidence_cutoff` when every page reached it uncapped, otherwise earlier. T is never later than `evidence_cutoff` or than the earliest material you did not read.
   - Each retrieval you stopped while it was still `capped`, or that was truncated, skipped or hit a tool limit, goes into `omissions` as {reason, from}, with from = that retrieval's `unread_from` (the earliest unread material). If you can't tell where the unread material starts, give the reason without from, and leave covered_through out: the checkpoint stays put.
   - A `since=` search or reaching a tool limit alone never justifies advancing covered_through; only material you read does.
   - A partial dream passes the T it actually reached, or omits covered_through so the next dream starts from the same point.
   - Coverage is contiguous. Reading the newest material first doesn't move T past an older gap: T stays at the end of the unbroken run you read from the previous checkpoint (or doesn't move), and the gap goes into `omissions` with its `from`. A useful lesson from today's material is still worth keeping even when T stays put. Never claim lifetime coverage, and never delete raw history.
   - The review says what you read (counts and range), what you corrected or added, and what you left unread.

Private reflections about yourself and colleagues stay in your memory or views; only project lessons go to project logs, through the project tools.
